# Propose unrequested work from observed activity under governed standing policies

This MasterPlan is a living document. The sections Progress, Surprises & Discoveries,
Decision Log, and Outcomes & Retrospective must be kept up to date as work proceeds.
If durable project context changes, update or create ADRs in docs/adr/ in the same change.


## Vision & Scope

Deliver [IR-21](../improvement-requests/propose-unrequested-work-from-observed-activity.md). After this initiative an operator declares one more agent, `agents/proof/proposer.dhall` promoted into `agents/`, whose trigger is a stream match on `activity.v1` (today the Intercom and transcript channels that `mori://shinzui/kanmon` publishes, plus a chat fixture source), whose behavior is the compiled Flow `propose-work`, and whose principal has an *observer allowance* in process configuration: the sources it may read, a resource envelope for each evaluation, and limits on evaluations, proposals and notifications per window. When a matching envelope arrives, the stream consumer extracts a bounded excerpt through a closed per-source adapter, runs a deterministic pre-filter, and either records a bounded skip in the proposal ledger (notifying nobody and starting no run) or enrolls a resource root with the evaluation envelope and enqueues one run. The run's Flow re-derives the same candidate from the journaled envelope, checks the ledger for an existing owner (an open proposal for the same source subject, an accepted proposal whose objective is still open, or a suppressed rejection) and for the allowance's windows, and then asks one registered typed Shikumi decision whether the excerpt describes actionable work. The decision's output is validated before it means anything: it may choose only a repository the policy supplied, only an action class the policy permits, and only evidence references that were in its input, and it can name no recipient and no authority. A validated assessment becomes a durable *proposal* keyed by observer principal and source subject, so redelivery, restart and several people restating one problem converge on one row. By default the Flow parks the proposal behind the existing durable approval child with the proposal's own expiry; the ask names the agent, says nobody requested the work, links its evidence and shows the accept, reject and stop commands. Accepting creates one objective under the agent's existing default-deny creation grant with a trusted origin link to the proposal and its source; rejecting records a suppression period; silence expires the proposal. Only when an operator has installed a *standing policy*, a revocable durable row naming the principal, sources, repositories, action classes, coordinator agent, per-objective and total envelopes, admission rate and expiry, does the Flow admit a proposal without a human: it reserves against the policy's totals, creates the objective, funds an independent coordinator run with the per-objective envelope, staffs the objective's single assignment and enqueues the coordinator, whose declared workspace and sink grants are unchanged by the policy and whose objective template stops at a reviewable artifact. Operators list candidates, proposals, decisions, policies and admissions through `shikigami proposals`, see counts and refusals as metrics, and see an exhausted limit as a visible stopped state rather than silence. Nothing in observed text, in a message author's identity or in model output can create, select or widen authority at any stage.

Out of scope, as the request states: automatic merging or deployment, a shared router that handles all incoming events, learning per-person preferences, and unbounded ambient monitoring. New intake providers (GitHub, pull requests) are a separate request; this initiative observes only what `activity.v1` already carries and qualifies from fixtures and existing channels. Making an admitted objective succeed is the adaptive coordinator's job under [plan 86](../plans/86-qualify-integrated-adaptive-autonomous-delivery.md); this initiative proves that admission produces one correctly funded, staffed and bounded objective, not that the objective completes. Watching admitted objectives for stuck or drifting work belongs to [IR-19](../improvement-requests/supervise-active-work-with-an-independent-watchdog-agent.md) and [MasterPlan 19](19-supervise-active-work-with-an-independent-watchdog-agent.md), which observes objectives admitted here like any others.


## Decomposition Strategy

Five children in three phases. Phase A is EP-1, the bounded-observation slice: the shared vocabulary, the observer allowance settings, the closed source adapters and deterministic pre-filter, the proposal ledger with its owner and window checks, and the stream-consumer change that records skips or enrolls an evaluation root inside the existing inbox transaction. Phase B runs two children against EP-1's types: EP-2 is the registered typed assessment decision with the validator that makes model output incapable of selecting repository, authority or recipient, and EP-3 is the `propose-work` Flow with its `Initiative` capability effect, the agent-run patch, the per-proposal approval wait and the objective creation on acceptance. Phase C is EP-4, standing policies and autonomous admission, followed by EP-5, which makes the ledger observable to operators, exports metrics, writes the guides and qualifies all nine IR-21 acceptance cases together before closing the request.

Observation is separate from assessment because the two have different owners and different proofs. EP-1 edits the trigger intake (`shikigami-workers/src/Shikigami/Trigger/Consumer.hs`) and owns a migration, and its acceptance is about bounds and atomicity: an irrelevant message leaves one skip row and no run, a relevant one leaves one root and one job in the same transaction as the inbox row, and two restatements of one problem converge on one proposal row. EP-2 is a registered program plus a validator that can be proved with the stub provider and property tests before any Flow calls it, and it owns the one shared-interpreter change (root-accounted decision slots) that MasterPlan 19's drift decision also needs. The Flow (EP-3) is separate from both because it is the only child that changes a declared durable body (`domain/shikigami/agent-run.keiro`) under [workflow versioning](../workflow-versioning.md) and the only child that parks on the approval child; keeping it in one child keeps one patch id, one conformance expectation edit and one acceptance story (acceptance 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6). Standing policies (EP-4) are separate because autonomous admission is the part of the request with the most authority at stake and the request itself says the default is human acceptance; EP-4 can be reviewed, deployed and revoked independently, and its policy store and commands can be built against EP-1's types while EP-3 is still in progress. Observability and qualification (EP-5) is its own child because the operator surface and metrics need real proposals and admissions to read, and because IR-21 closure must not be claimed before every acceptance case has evidence from the integrated path.

Alternatives considered. One ExecPlan was rejected: intake, a typed program, a Flow with an approval wait, a policy store and an operator surface would hide that the Flow cannot be built before the vocabulary and the decision exist, and would make autonomous admission inseparable from the human path. One child per IR-21 acceptance item was rejected because acceptance items cut across the same modules (items 1, 2, 4 and 6 are all the Flow; items 3, 8 and 9 are all the policy). Evaluating candidates in the consumer with a model call was rejected because the consumer must acknowledge a broker offset after exactly one durable disposition ([ADR 0009](../adr/0009-kafka-offsets-advance-only-after-durable-dispositions.md)) and a provider call there would be neither journaled nor replay-safe; only the pure pre-filter runs at intake. Running the pre-filter only inside the Flow was rejected because every matching message would then cost a workflow, which the request's attention and spend bounds argue against; the Flow still re-derives the candidate from the journaled envelope so no run trusts intake for authority. Starting admitted work as a delegated child of the proposer's run was rejected because `enrollRootRun` refuses descendants and the proposer's root is sized for one evaluation; an independent coordinator run with its own root keeps the per-objective envelope separate from observation spend. A new workflow family instead of a Flow inside the agent run was rejected because the request asks for an agent observing a trigger, and the agent run already supplies the recorded principal, the journaled approval policy, the objective creation scope and the sink path. Naming the concept root `Proposal` was rejected because `Shikigami.Coordination.Proposal` already names the adaptive coordinator's organization proposals; the root is `Initiative`, the records keep the request's words (candidate, proposal, standing policy, admission), and the operator noun is `proposals`.

Research against `88926307` verified every module, symbol and ADR the children cite. ## Integration Points

**Vocabulary — EP-1 owns; EP-2, EP-3, EP-4 and EP-5 consume.** `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Initiative/Types.hs` defines, following the house value rules: allocated identities `ProposalId` and `StandingPolicyId` as private newtypes over `KindID` with the singular prefixes `shikigami_proposal` and `shikigami_standing_policy`, minted as UUIDv7 only in IO by the owning ledger step and admitted through checked parsers with no `Generic` and no record selector; the closed sums `SourceKind` (`IntercomConversation | MeetingTranscript | ChatFixture`), `ActionClass` (`ReproduceAndDiagnose | DraftFix | DraftDocumentation`, with no publishing constructor by design), `SkipReason`, `ProposalStatus` and `RefusalKind`; the checked `Excerpt` (1 to 8192 characters, no control characters, redacted `Show`), `ExcerptDigest`, `Confidence` (finite, 0 to 1), `OutcomeText` and `CriterionText`; the structured `EvidenceRef` (activity id, source subject, occurrence instant and excerpt digest, never a body); `ProposalKey` as an `ExecutionDigest "proposal-key"` computed by `proposalKeyFor :: AgentPrincipal -> ObservedSource -> ProposalKey` over a versioned canonical object (`shikigami.proposal-key/1`); the sealed `Candidate` minted only by `Initiative.Prefilter.prefilter`; the sealed `Assessment` whose constructor is private and whose only minter is EP-2's validator; the `RequestedAuthority` record (repository, action class, envelope); the scoped `Duration "initiative-window"`, `Duration "initiative-proposal"`, `Duration "initiative-suppression"`, `Duration "initiative-evaluation"` and `Duration "initiative-objective"` from `Shikigami.Duration`; the positive `InitiativeLimit (scope :: Symbol)` modelled on `Inspection.Settings.InspectionLimit`; the revisions `StateRevision "proposal"` and `StateRevision "standing-policy"`; and the public `ProposalView`, `CandidateView` and `AssessmentView` read records, ordinary `Generic` records with explicit codecs. The `ObservedSource` structured coordinate (source, subject kind, subject id as three `ActivityCoordinate`s) and the nominal `ProposalRef` locator live in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Objective/Keys.hs` because the objective owner must carry them without depending on `Initiative`; EP-1 adds them there. Later children add fields only additively and never widen a closed sum without recording the change here.

**Observer allowance settings — EP-1 owns; EP-3, EP-4 and EP-5 consume.** `Shikigami.Initiative.Settings` defines `InitiativeSettings {observers :: [ObserverAllowance]}` and `ObserverAllowance` (principal, permitted `SourceKind`s, minimum excerpt length, `EvaluationEnvelope` of dimension limits and cutoff window, `evaluationsPerWindow`, `proposalsPerWindow`, `notificationsPerWindow`, `window`, `proposalExpiry`, `rejectionSuppression`, and a `NoticeTarget` for admission notices). It is an optional object in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/App/Config.hs` under the key `initiatives` (environment binding `SHIKIGAMI_INITIATIVES`), loaded like the objective settings with every key checked and every limit positive. Omission disables observation: the consumer treats every agent as having no allowance and the Flow journals `NoObserverAllowance`. An allowance is attention and spend authority only; it is not objective creation authority (that stays in `objectives.agentCreation`) and not admission authority (that is a standing policy). `resolveAllowance :: InitiativeSettings -> AgentIdentity -> Either SkipReason ObserverAllowance` is the single lookup both the consumer and the Flow use.

**Source adapters and the pre-filter — EP-1 owns; EP-3 consumes, EP-2 consumes the output type.** `Shikigami.Initiative.Source.observe :: UTCTime -> ActivityEnvelope -> Either SkipReason Observed` recognises a source by its `source` coordinate (`external/intercom`, `external/transcripts`, `fixture/chat`) and extracts the excerpt from the payload path that channel documents, refusing agent actors and unknown sources. `Shikigami.Initiative.Prefilter.prefilter :: ObserverAllowance -> Observed -> Either SkipReason Candidate` is total and pure, and the consumer and the Flow call the same function so their verdicts cannot disagree. `Shikigami.Initiative.Intake.disposition` composes allowance, adapter and pre-filter into the one call the stream consumer makes.

**Stream intake — EP-1 owns the additions; the Trigger vertical keeps its consumer.** `prepareActivityHits` in `shikigami-workers/src/Shikigami/Trigger/Consumer.hs` calls `Initiative.Intake.disposition` for each matched agent after identity admission. A skip writes one `initiative_candidates` row through `Ledger.recordSkipTx` inside the inbox transaction and enqueues nothing; a candidate allocates a root id in IO before the transaction, inserts the root origin and enrolls the root through a new `Shikigami.Execution.ResourcesStore.allocateRootForWorkflowTx` (factored from `allocateRootForWorkflow`, same binding and conflict rules) with the allowance's evaluation envelope and a cutoff of the receipt instant plus the evaluation window, and enqueues the job with `executionRootId = Just root`. Agents without an allowance produce byte-identical jobs. The early producer/consumer check, owned by EP-1 milestone 3, drives `processDecodedActivity` with a fixture envelope for an observer agent and proves one inbox row, one root origin, one root and one job commit together, and that a store failure after the root insert rolls all of them back.

**Proposal ledger — EP-1 owns; EP-3, EP-4 and EP-5 consume.** The next free migration number (the manifest ends at `0042`; MasterPlan 19 may take numbers first) creates `shikigami.initiative_candidates` (primary key observer principal and activity id; verdict, skip reason, proposal id, run workflow id, recorded instant; no body text), `shikigami.initiative_proposals` (allocated `ProposalId`, `proposal_key`, observer, source subject, assessment view, requested authority, status, instants, revision; a partial unique index on `proposal_key` where status is `pending`) and `shikigami.initiative_observers` (one anchor row per observer principal used only as a lock for window counting). `Shikigami.Initiative.Ledger` exposes `recordSkipTx`, `recordCandidateTx`, `candidateRecordedTx`, `markEvaluationStartedTx`, `markCandidateProposedTx`, `checkOwner`, `checkWindows`, `recordProposal` (converging on the pending row for the key, discarding a losing allocated id, per [ADR 0073](../adr/0073-objective-identity-is-allocated-and-creation-receipts-own-deduplication.md)), `resolveProposal`, `readProposal`, `readProposalByKey` and bounded keyset readers that EP-5 extends. Every instant written is supplied by the caller from a journaled step value, never `now()` inside a Flow step. Window counting is a bounded indexed count over rows with instants inside the window, serialized by the observer anchor row lock.

**Assessment decision — EP-2 owns; EP-3 declares the slot and steps.** EP-2 adds the `Propose` constructor to `DecisionLabel` in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Flow/Decision.hs` with slot label `propose` and step names `decision-prepare-propose` and `decision-result-propose`, registers `assessmentProgram` in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Program/Decision/Registry.hs` under program id `shikigami.initiative-assessment`, and defines `AssessmentInput` (the excerpt as quoted evidence, the evidence references, the candidate repositories as an indexed closed list, the permitted action classes) and the model-facing `AssessmentOutput`, whose validator `validateAssessment :: AssessmentInput -> AssessmentOutput -> Either DecisionFailure Assessment` is the only minter of `Assessment` and requires the chosen repository index to be in range, the action class to be permitted, every cited evidence reference to be in the input, confidence to be finite and bounded, and text fields to be within bounds. The output type has no recipient, principal, authority or operation field, so nothing in it can be mistaken for one. EP-3 declares the two decision steps inside the `shikigami-initiative-v1` patch, lists the slot in the `propose-work` `FlowDef` and lists the alias `propose` in `agents/proof/proposer.dhall` `decisionModels`.

## Surprises & Discoveries

- A resource root admits a model attempt only when `ActiveWork`, `ModelAttempts`, `Tokens` and `CostMicroUsd` are all named, and a finite cost cannot be reserved before a call. The evaluation envelope rule, and EP-4's per-objective validation, therefore require all four rather than `ModelAttempts` alone.
- `Dimension`'s map keys encode as arrays of pairs, so operator documents use `Initiative.Settings.parseLimits` and `renderLimits` (`{"ModelAttempts": 1, "Tokens": "unbounded"}`). EP-4's policy document reuses them.
- A registered program's result validator cannot see its input, so every choice rule lives in `validateAssessment`, called by the Flow; a bad answer is a recorded skip rather than a failed run.
- FRIC-13's collector crash recurred (FRIC-60): constructing `AgentRunDeps` by a record literal in an optimized test module segfaults at the first major GC, for a reason not established. The fixture and `Shikigami.Workers.Config` are `-O0`. Production's construction is not exercised by any test, so its exposure is unknown.
- The admission plan omitted registering the coordinator run's objective association, without which its `objectives-policy` is denied. EP-4 does it in the enqueue transaction.
- The combined suite command in every child plan exhausts PostgreSQL, and workspace Git cases race at four threads (FRIC-59). Children run each suite alone.


## Decision Log

- Decision: The deterministic pre-filter runs at the stream trigger and again in the Flow; only the Flow evaluates with a model.
  Rationale: The consumer acknowledges an offset after exactly one durable disposition (ADR 0009), so a provider call there would be unjournaled and unsafe to replay, while a pure pre-filter over the matched envelope is both cheap and safe. Recording a skip at intake means an irrelevant message costs one bounded row and no workflow, which is what the request's attention bounds ask for. The Flow re-derives the candidate from the journaled envelope with the same pure functions so a run never trusts intake for authority.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: A proposal is keyed by observer principal and source subject, and its receipt owns deduplication.
  Rationale: The activity id differs for every restatement of one problem, and model output is not a stable key. The source subject (the Intercom conversation, the transcript page, the fixture chat) is the one coordinate that redelivery, restart and restatement share. A pending proposal for a key is the owner; a losing allocated id is discarded exactly as ADR 0073 does for objectives.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Observation spend is a per-run resource root enrolled at the trigger from an observer allowance in process configuration; attention is a ledger window per observer.
  Rationale: Roots are the house accounting entity and bound actual provider calls; `enrollRootRun` refuses descendants, so the root must be created by the producer, and the inbox transaction is the only place that makes it one fact with the job. Windows need counts, not reservations, and a bounded indexed count under an anchor row lock is simpler than a second accounting owner. Keeping the allowance in configuration rather than in the agent declaration keeps attention and spend with the operator and leaves MasterPlan 19's declared schedule envelope untouched.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Model output is validated into a sealed `Assessment` that can only choose among supplied repositories, permitted action classes and input evidence references, and that has no recipient, principal or authority field.
  Rationale: ADR 0029 makes model output a proposal until validated against journaled authority. Giving the output type no way to express authority makes acceptance 4 structural rather than a filter that a prompt could evade.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: The proposing Flow lives inside the agent run behind `shikigami-initiative-v1`, parks on the existing approval child with a per-proposal expiry, and creates the objective under the proposer's existing creation grant.
  Rationale: The agent run already supplies the recorded principal, the journaled approval policy, the Flow objective scope with default-deny creation and the sink path. The approval child already gives a stable review id, an opaque token and `shikigami resolve-approval`. Adding one deadline-carrying acknowledgement operation is smaller than a new wait primitive.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Standing policies are revocable durable rows installed by the configured local operator, read fresh under a row lock at every admission, and pinned by digest in the admission receipt.
  Rationale: Revocation must stop new admission immediately, which a journaled snapshot cannot do, while an admitted objective must be able to prove which policy admitted it. A durable row with a revision and a digest serves both. Installing an agent or Skill never writes such a row.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Admission funds and staffs an independent coordinator run with its own root rather than delegating a child.
  Rationale: Descendants cannot enroll roots and inherit the parent's envelope, which here is sized for one evaluation. An addressed run job with its own root, an objective association and a deterministic key is how the adaptive fixture already starts a coordinator, and it keeps the per-objective envelope separate from observation spend.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Decision slots declare their accounting through a total function on `DecisionLabel`; only the new `Propose` label (and MasterPlan 19's `Drift`) is root-accounted.
  Rationale: Accounting every synchronous Flow decision would change existing Flows that run under roots and could refuse fixtures that never declared model attempts. A closed total function keeps the change additive and reviewable, and the two initiatives share one mechanism.
  Date: 2026-11-04
