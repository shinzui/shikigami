# Supervise active work with an independent watchdog agent

This MasterPlan is a living document. The sections Progress, Surprises & Discoveries,
Decision Log, and Outcomes & Retrospective must be kept up to date as work proceeds.
If durable project context changes, update or create ADRs in docs/adr/ in the same change.


## Vision & Scope

Deliver [IR-19](../improvement-requests/supervise-active-work-with-an-independent-watchdog-agent.md). After this initiative an operator enables one more agent, declared in the proof catalog as `agents/proof/watchdog.dhall` and promoted into `agents/`, with its own Meibo principal and a cron trigger. Every tick the existing scheduler enqueues an ordinary agent run whose body is a compiled Flow called `watchdog-sweep`. The sweep reads a bounded page of the active population (open objectives and their coordinators, worker sessions in `Active`, `Parked` or `Unresolved`, executions in `Active`, `Parked` or `Unresolved`, their roots and unsettled workspace processes) through lock-free, body-free observation reads under an explicit observation grant. It compares each subject's activity mark (the owner revisions and cursors that advance only when work is accepted) with the mark the watchdog itself recorded on earlier sweeps, and classifies the subject deterministically as progressing, legitimately waiting (a pending approval or tool permission within its deadline, a command within its absolute deadline, a parked assignment within the configured grace), unresolved (an uncertain operation outcome, never called failed), stuck, or a stuck coordinator (an open objective whose coordinator waits for a checkpoint that no idle worker will ever publish, or whose coordinator run is absent). Progressing subjects that are due are assessed for drift by one bounded typed Shikumi decision per sweep, compared against the objective's goal and criteria metadata; its answer is advisory and cannot invent evidence or choose its subject. Findings are typed, coalesce into one episode per subject and detection kind, and produce a further notice only when the evidence summary changes materially. Notices leave through the watchdog's declared sinks, addressed to the objective's bound conversation when one exists and otherwise to the operator channel. The watchdog's model calls charge a resource root enrolled for its own run from a declared envelope, so an exhausted objective cannot silence it and observation never spends an objective's budget. Operators see the last completed sweep, its coverage of the population, refusals and open episodes through `shikigami watchdog inspect --json`, through exported metrics, and through an overdue verdict with a nonzero exit code. The watchdog holds no intervention authority: a cancel, transfer or fence attempted under its identity is refused by the existing objective and worker command owners, and nothing in worker or model output can change a finding's subject, recipient or the watchdog's authority.

Out of scope, as the request states: automatic remediation, general-purpose alerting, quality scoring of finished work, and choosing default windows for real workloads (every window is configurable and every finding reports its observation limit). Recurring mandates ([IR-18](../improvement-requests/support-recurring-objective-supervision.md), plan 87) are not a dependency; the watchdog observes them as objectives once they exist. Machine daemons ([IR-20](../improvement-requests/execute-governed-work-through-machine-daemons.md)) and unrequested-work proposals ([IR-21](../improvement-requests/propose-unrequested-work-from-observed-activity.md)) are separate requests; IR-21 states that it expects IR-19 to observe objectives, which this initiative satisfies. Reading transcripts, instruction bodies, result bodies or repository contents is excluded by design, not deferred.


## Decomposition Strategy

Five children in three phases. Phase A is EP-1, the observation slice: the shared watchdog vocabulary, the observation grant, and additive lock-free observation reads in each owner vertical, composed into one bounded observation page with a coverage cursor. Phase B runs two independent children against EP-1's types: EP-2 is the deterministic stuck classifier with the mark, episode and sweep ledger, and EP-3 is the drift decision program with its deterministic provider and cadence. Phase C is EP-4, the declared agent itself (the Flow body, the `Observe` capability effect, the agent-run patch, the declared resource envelope enrolled per tick, batched sink delivery and the authority refusal proofs), followed by EP-5, which makes the watchdog observable to operators, qualifies all eight IR-19 acceptance cases together and closes the request.

The observation slice is separate from classification because the two have different owners and different proofs. EP-1 edits `Shikigami.Objective.Store`, `Shikigami.WorkerSession.Store`, `Shikigami.Execution.Store`, `Shikigami.Execution.ResourcesStore`, `Shikigami.Workspace.ProcessStore` and `Shikigami.Coordination.Sql`, and its acceptance is about contention and bounds: a sweep page completes while a competing transaction holds `FOR UPDATE` on an objective, and never returns an instruction, result or transcript body. EP-2 is pure Haskell over EP-1's records plus one application-owned ledger, and its acceptance is about verdicts and idempotence under restart. Drift (EP-3) is separate from stuck detection because it adds a model call with its own cadence, limit and accounting, touches the decision core (`Shikigami.Flow.Decision`'s closed `DecisionLabel` and `Shikigami.Program.Decision.Registry`), and must be provable with the stub provider before any Flow invokes it. The agent (EP-4) is separate from all three because it is the only child that changes a declared durable body (`domain/shikigami/agent-run.keiro`) and must follow [workflow versioning](../workflow-versioning.md); keeping that change in one child keeps one patch id and one conformance expectation edit. Observability and qualification (EP-5) is its own child because the operator surface, metrics and the integrated acceptance runner need EP-4's real sweeps, and because IR-19 closure must not be claimed before every acceptance case has evidence.

Alternatives considered. One ExecPlan was rejected: five functional concerns across core, workers and server, a declared-body change and a model decision would hide the dependency that the agent cannot be built before the reads and the classifier exist. One child per IR-19 acceptance item was rejected because acceptance items cut across the same modules (items 1, 2 and 3 are all the classifier; items 5, 6 and 7 are all the agent). Folding EP-2 into EP-1 was rejected because the classifier can be property-checked with no database while the reads cannot be checked without one. Folding drift into EP-4 was rejected because it would make the first sweep wait for the decision core change and the stub-provider proof. A separate `shikigami.watchdog` workflow family instead of a Flow inside the agent run was rejected because the request asks for a declared agent on the existing schedule trigger, and the agent-run workflow already supplies the recorded principal, lifecycle rechecks, journaled policy snapshots, sink dispatch and the per-tick workflow identity. A worker loop or sleep loop was rejected by the request itself and by [ADR 0016](../adr/0016-a-cron-agent-runs-about-now-not-once-per-missed-minute.md). Measuring staleness from owner timestamps was rejected after research: only `shikigami.objectives`, `shikigami.worker_sessions` and `shikigami.executions` carry an `updated_at` column, two different clock functions write them, and the facts that matter (messages, events, decisions, attempts, processes) carry revisions and cursors only.

Research against `0c246a62` verified every module, recipe, symbol and ADR the children cite. The facts that shaped the decomposition: `Objective.Store.read`, `inspect`, `ResourcesStore.inspectRoot` and `ProcessStore.inspectProcessTx` take row locks and `listObjectives` takes `FOR SHARE`, while `WorkerSession.Store.inspectSession`, `readEvents` and `Execution.Store.inspectExecution*` are lock-free; nothing enumerates executions, roots or approvals. `SessionState` has no heartbeat or last-activity field; `Parked` has no window. Pending human approvals exist only as a `shikigami.approval` child workflow with a Keiro timer of kind `shikigami.approval-timeout`; the one durable absolute deadline is `Operation.permission.expiresAt` while an execution is `Parked`, and a workspace command's absolute deadline is `ProcessRecord.deadline`. A coordinator waits through an `adaptive_checkpoint_listeners` row whose listener carries `after :: StateRevision "objective"`, decides through `adaptive_decisions` rows joined to objective receipts, and stops only through an `adaptive_observations` row of kind `terminal`; plan 85 reflects on findings, never on silence. `resolveGovernedCapabilityOverlays` refuses any non-Skill behavior, so a Flow reaches data only through an effect in `FlowEffects`, which [ADR 0013](../adr/0013-flow-bodies-reach-the-world-only-through-capabilities.md) says to extend with a capability, following `Shikigami.PracticeReview.Capability`. `enrollRootRun` is called only from tests; every production producer sets `executionRootId = Nothing`; `AgentDecl` has no envelope field. The scheduled run's workflow id derives from `scheduleRunIdempotencyKey agent dueAt`, so a restart mid-sweep replays the same journal. `DecisionLabel` is the closed sum `Triage | Investigation | Judgment | Adaptive`, decision steps are declared in `domain/shikigami/agent-run.keiro`, and `runDecisionAccepted` accepts only `Adaptive`. There is no controllable clock abstraction; pure functions take `UTCTime` and tests use fixed instants. Metrics are OpenTelemetry instruments on `ShikigamiMetrics`, named `shikigami.<noun>.<verb>`, with no run ids or error text as attributes. Mori discovery found no registered project owning stuck or drift detection for agent work.

## Dependency Graph

```text
EP-1 (109) ---+---> EP-2 (110) ---+
              |                   +---> EP-4 (112) ---> EP-5 (113)
              +---> EP-3 (111) ---+
                     ^   (soft) EP-2 ledger for drift episodes

EP-2 and EP-3 proceed in parallel once EP-1 is Complete.
EP-4 is the integration child: it is the only child that edits a declared
workflow body and the only child that enqueues real scheduled runs.
```

EP-2 requires EP-1 because the classifier consumes EP-1's observation records and activity marks, and because the sealed `Finding` and `EvidenceRef` types it mints are defined by EP-1 so that EP-2 and EP-3 can agree on them without waiting for each other. EP-3 requires EP-1 for the same vocabulary and for the body-free objective metadata (goal, criteria, assignment states, accepted operation specifications and artifact references) that is the only admissible drift input. EP-4 requires EP-1, EP-2 and EP-3 because its Flow body is the composition of all three: it journals an observation page, classifies it, records marks and episodes, runs the drift slot and delivers findings; declaring the agent-run patch before the slot and the ledger exist would either leave the drift step unused or require a second patch. EP-5 requires EP-4 because inspection reads the ledger that real sweeps write, metrics are recorded by the capability interpreter, and the acceptance runner drives real scheduled ticks.

No child waits on plan 86, plan 87 or any other open plan. EP-1 may begin immediately. If EP-2 completes before EP-3, EP-4 may start its Flow skeleton and observation and ledger steps, but it must not declare the agent-run patch or the `watchdog-sweep` slot inventory until EP-3 is Complete, because the patch body is the one place the drift steps are declared.


## Integration Points

**Watchdog vocabulary — EP-1 owns; EP-2, EP-3, EP-4 and EP-5 consume.** `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Watchdog/Types.hs` defines, following the house value rules: allocated identities `SweepId`, `EpisodeId` and `FindingId` as private newtypes over `KindID` with the singular prefixes `shikigami_watchdog_sweep`, `shikigami_watchdog_episode` and `shikigami_watchdog_finding`, minted as UUIDv7 only by the owning ledger or journaled step and admitted through checked parsers; the closed `Subject` sum (`ObjectiveCoordinator ObjectiveId`, `WorkerSession WorkerSessionId`, `Execution ExecutionId`) with a stable `SubjectKey` rendering for storage; the per-kind `ActivityMark` records whose fields are exactly the owner revisions and cursors that advance when work is accepted, with `markDigest :: ActivityMark -> MarkDigest` over a versioned canonical JSON object (`shikigami.watchdog-mark/1`); the closed `WaitingReason` (`AwaitingApproval {until, review}`, `AwaitingPermission {until}`, `RunningCommand {until, operationId}`, `ParkedAssignment {since}`, `ObjectiveBlocked`), `Verdict` (`Progressing`, `Waiting WaitingReason`, `Unresolved`, `Stuck`, `StuckCoordinator`, `Terminal`) and `DetectionKind` (`StuckWork`, `StuckCoordinator`, `UnresolvedOutcome`, `Drift`) sums; the closed `EvidenceRef` sum of structured coordinates that never carry bodies (objective revision, assignment revision and owner epoch, session event cursor, execution operation position, adaptive decision operation, root revision and usage, process operation id, approval review id); `ObservedWindow {since, until}` and `ObservationLimit` (the first sweep that saw the subject); the scoped windows `Duration "watchdog-stuck"`, `Duration "watchdog-parked"`, `Duration "watchdog-coordinator"` and `Duration "watchdog-drift"` from `Shikigami.Duration`; the bounded `WatchdogLimit (scope :: Symbol)` positive page limits modelled on `Inspection.Settings.InspectionLimit`; and the public `FindingView` read record, an ordinary `Generic` record with an explicit codec. The sealed `Finding` itself is defined in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Watchdog/Episode.hs` by EP-2, which alone mints it: a private constructor, no `Generic`, no context-free `FromJSON`, and `findingView :: Finding -> FindingView` as the only way out (ADR 0081's rule that a seal lives with its validator). Later children add fields only additively and never widen a closed sum without recording the change here.

**Observation grant and settings — EP-1 owns; EP-4 and EP-5 consume.** `Shikigami.Watchdog.Grant` defines the sealed `ObservationGrant` (watchdog principal, `ObservationScope` of `AllObjectives` or a non-empty objective list, windows and per-sweep budget) and `resolveObservationGrant :: WatchdogSettings -> AgentIdentity -> Either GrantRefusal ObservationGrant`, which refuses any identity other than the configured watchdog principal, plus the journalable `GrantSnapshot` (scope, windows and budget without the principal) with `snapshotGrant` and `restoreGrant :: GrantSnapshot -> AgentIdentity -> WatchdogSettings -> Either GrantRefusal ObservationGrant`, so a sweep pins the windows it was admitted with and rechecks only the principal on replay. `WatchdogSettings` is a new optional object in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/App/Config.hs` and its Settei loader (`watchdog`, environment binding `SHIKIGAMI_WATCHDOG`), with EP-1 owning `principal`, `scope`, `stuckWindowSeconds`, `parkedWindowSeconds`, `coordinatorWindowSeconds` and `subjectsPerSweep`; EP-3 adds `driftIntervalSeconds` and `driftSubjectsPerSweep`; EP-4 adds `operatorRecipient` and `conversationSink`. Omission disables the watchdog: an agent run with the `watchdog-sweep` Flow then journals a refusal and emits nothing. A grant is read authority only; it is not objective membership, carries no command permission, and has no JSON decoder.

**Owner observation reads — EP-1 owns the additions; the owner verticals keep their SQL and decoders.** Each owner exposes one additive, lock-free, keyset-paged, body-free read that returns an owner-defined observation record rather than its state: `Objective.Store.observeOpenObjectives`, `WorkerSession.Store.observeOpenSessions`, `Execution.Store.observeOpenExecutions`, `Execution.ResourcesStore.observeRoots`, `Workspace.ProcessStore.observeUnsettledProcesses` and `Coordination.Sql.observeCoordinators`. None uses `FOR UPDATE` or `FOR SHARE`; each runs inside a read-only transaction with `SET LOCAL statement_timeout` and `lock_timeout` supplied by the caller; each decodes stored JSON through the owner's existing decoders and refuses corruption with the owner's error inside that transaction. `Shikigami.Watchdog.Observation` composes them into `ObservationPage {observedAt, cursor, subjects, refusals}` with set-based follow-up reads (executions by session, processes by operation, roots by id) so a sweep performs a bounded number of statements, not one per subject. Pending human approvals are read through Keiro's public workflow header and pending-timer reads that the inspection host already mounts (`shikigami-core/inspection/Shikigami/Inspection/Catalog.hs`), filtered to timer kind `shikigami.approval-timeout`; private Keiro tables are never queried. The early producer/consumer check, owned by EP-1 milestone 2, holds `FOR UPDATE` on an objective row and a session row from a second connection and proves the page completes within its statement timeout and contains both subjects.

**Marks, episodes, findings and the sweep ledger — EP-2 owns; EP-3, EP-4 and EP-5 consume.** Migration `0043-watchdog-ledger` creates `shikigami.watchdog_sweeps`, `shikigami.watchdog_marks`, `shikigami.watchdog_episodes`, `shikigami.watchdog_findings` and the `shikigami.watchdog_coverage` singleton (cursor and pass boundaries), all schema-qualified, with a partial unique index on open episodes. Every timestamp in these tables is the page's journaled `observedAt` supplied by the caller, never `now()`. `Shikigami.Watchdog.Store` exposes `openSweep`, `recordSweepPage`, `completePass`, `closeSweep`, `readPriorMarks`, `readCoverage`, `writeCoverage` and, from EP-5, bounded readers; identities are allocated in IO and passed in through an `IdentityPool`, and every write is keyed by `SweepId` and `SubjectKey` so a replayed step converges on the same rows. `Shikigami.Watchdog.Episode` owns the coalescing rule: an episode opens on the first non-progressing verdict for a subject and detection kind, each sweep records an `EvidenceSummary` whose digest excludes elapsed time and sweep identity, and a `Finding` is minted only when no open episode exists or the summary digest differs from the last notified one. EP-3 records drift results through the same `recordSweepPage` with `DetectionKind` `Drift`, adds `driftCategory` to EP-2's `EvidenceSummary` additively, and owns its own `watchdog_drift_checks` cadence table in migration `0044`. EP-4 writes the ledger only through journaled capability steps. EP-5 reads it for inspection and metrics.

**Classifier contract — EP-2 owns; EP-4 consumes.** `Shikigami.Watchdog.Classify.classify :: Windows -> UTCTime -> Maybe PriorMark -> SubjectObservation -> Verdict` is total and pure. The `UTCTime` is the page's journaled `observedAt`, never a fresh clock read inside the Flow. `PriorMark {digest, firstSeenAt, lastChangedAt}` comes from `watchdog_marks`. The coordinator rule is independent of worker verdicts: an open objective with a `Coordinator` member, no `terminal` adaptive observation, and either no coordinator run and no listener, or a listener whose `after` revision is already satisfied while every assignment session is `Idle` with nothing queued and no execution active, whose mark is unchanged for the coordinator window, is `StuckCoordinator`.

**Drift decision — EP-3 owns; EP-4 declares the slot and steps.** EP-3 adds the `Drift` constructor to `DecisionLabel` in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Flow/Decision.hs` with `slotLabel` `drift` and step names `decision-prepare-drift` and `decision-result-drift`, registers `driftProgram` in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Program/Decision/Registry.hs` under program id `shikigami.watchdog-drift`, and defines `DriftInput` (one objective's goal, criteria metadata and up to `driftSubjectsPerSweep` subjects with their accepted operation specifications and artifact references as `EvidenceRef`s) and `DriftAssessment` (per subject a closed `DriftCategory` of `WithinScope | PossibleDrift | OutsideScope`, a bounded rationale and evidence references that the validator requires to be a subset of the input references). EP-4 declares the two steps inside the `shikigami-watchdog-v1` patch in `domain/shikigami/agent-run.keiro`, lists the slot in the `watchdog-sweep` `FlowDef`, and lists the alias in `agents/watchdog.dhall` `decisionModels`. Drift calls are accounted through `Shikigami.Decision.Accounting.accountedDecisionHooks` against the watchdog run's root; with no root the slot refuses rather than calling a provider.

**The agent, its Flow, the `Observe` capability and the patch — EP-4 owns.** `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Flow/Observe.hs` defines the `Observe` effect (open sweep, observe page, record page, record drift, deliver, close sweep) with a journaled interpreter modelled on `Shikigami.PracticeReview.Capability` (process-local runtime, explicit `unavailable` and `failClosed` runtimes), wired through `AgentRunDeps` in `shikigami-workers/src/Shikigami/Workers/Config.hs`. `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Flow/Watchdog.hs` is the `watchdog-sweep` `FlowDef` with `requiresDefaultModel = DefaultModelOptional`, registered only in `flowRegistry`. Its static step names (`watchdog-open`, `watchdog-grant`, `watchdog-observe`, `watchdog-record`, `watchdog-drift-record`, the batched sink step for label `watchdog-deliver`, `watchdog-close`, plus the two drift decision steps) are declared once in `agent-run.keiro` inside `patch shikigami-watchdog-v1`, registered through `Shikigami.Workflow.Versioning` and mirrored in `KeiroConformance/Expectations.hs`. One run is one sweep and one generation; the Flow never loops and never calls `continueAsNew`. `agents/proof/watchdog.dhall` is the reference declaration: the Flow, a cron trigger, `identity`, `decisionModels`, the new `resources` envelope and no run-end `sinks` (a run-end digest every tick would be noise); operators promote it into `agents/` as the operations guide (EP-5) describes. Delivery sinks are declared in `WatchdogSettings` (`operatorRecipient`, `conversationSink`), validated at load by `Sink.Validate.sinkDeclarationErrors`, and grant-checked per intent.

**Declared resource envelope for scheduled runs — EP-4 owns.** `AgentDecl` gains `resources :: Maybe ResourceEnvelopeDecl` (dimension limits and a cutoff window) in `shikigami-core/src/Shikigami/Agent/Decl.hs` and `schema/Agent.dhall`, defaulting to `Nothing` with the omitted-key codec rule of [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-defaulted-agent-fields-preserve-journal-codec-compatibility.md). `shikigami-workers/src/Shikigami/Trigger/Scheduler.hs` `scheduleFireAction` enrolls a root through `Shikigami.Execution.Admission.enrollRootRun` for a declaration that carries an envelope, with cutoff `dueAt` plus the window, before `enqueueScheduledRun`; declarations without an envelope are unchanged. Only the schedule path honors the field in this initiative; other producers are unchanged and documented as such.

## Decision Log

- Decision: Measure staleness against the watchdog's own sweep marks, not owner timestamps.
  Rationale: Only three owner tables carry `updated_at`, written by two different clock functions, and the facts that prove accepted work (messages, events, decisions, attempts, processes) carry revisions and cursors only. Comparing a subject's mark digest between sweeps is deterministic, needs no owner change, and makes the observation limit explicit in every finding.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Observation is a Flow capability effect served by additive lock-free owner reads under a read-only grant.
  Rationale: `resolveGovernedCapabilityOverlays` refuses non-Skill behaviors and ADR 0013 forbids widening `FlowEffects` except by a capability. Existing inspection reads lock rows and nothing enumerates the population, so a sweep against them would contend with writers; owner-exposed observation reads keep SQL and decoders with their owners while the watchdog owns composition and classification.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: The watchdog is a compiled Flow inside the existing agent-run workflow, one sweep per scheduled tick, declared behind one patch `shikigami-watchdog-v1`.
  Rationale: The request asks for a declared agent on the existing schedule trigger. The agent run already supplies the recorded principal, journaled policy snapshots, sink dispatch and a per-tick workflow identity that makes restart replay the same sweep. A new workflow family or loop would duplicate those owners.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Drift is one batched typed decision per sweep with its own `DecisionLabel`, bounded by `driftSubjectsPerSweep` and `driftInterval`, charged to the watchdog run's root.
  Rationale: A decision slot is one-use per generation and labels are static, so per-subject slots would need a declared label family. One bounded batch keeps the model call count at most one per sweep and makes the limit a configuration value rather than a declaration shape.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: A scheduled agent may declare a resource envelope that `scheduleFireAction` enrolls per tick; only the schedule path honors it in this initiative.
  Rationale: No production producer enrolls roots today and `AgentDecl` has no envelope field. The request requires watchdog usage to be separate from observed roots; a per-tick root keyed by the tick's workflow id is idempotent and bounded without a new accounting owner.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Watchdog liveness is an operator-facing sweep ledger, metrics and a command verdict, not a readiness or liveness probe.
  Rationale: ADR 0020 restricts readiness to what restarting or eviction fixes and liveness to in-process progress; a late sweep is neither. A stopped watchdog must still be distinguishable from "no findings", which the sweep age metric and `inspect --fail-when-overdue` provide without touching the probes.
  Date: 2026-11-04

- Decision: Findings leave only through the watchdog's declared sinks, addressed to the objective's bound conversation or the operator recipient; no mailbox write, objective command or checkpoint wake is used for delivery.
  Rationale: Delivering into a coordinator's mailbox or objective evidence is a command requiring authority the request says the watchdog must not hold by default, and acceptance 4 requires that no objective state changes. Bound conversations already name where an objective's people and coordinator converse.
  Date: 2026-11-04
